## Key facts to remember
- Qiskit is little-endian (qubit 0 is the rightmost bit); Lab 2 is big-endian. `reverse_qargs()` converts.
- Grover N=8 peaks at k=2 (0.9453), overshoots at k=3. Two marked items out of 8: k=1 gives probability exactly 1.
- Shor N=15: every coprime base works with probability 0.5 except a=14 (a^(r/2) = -1, never). With N=21, success is 0 until 2^t > N and is not monotone in t (0.012 at t=5, 0.005 at t=6, 0.31 at t=9).
- Bit-flip code: logical/raw error ratio is 3p - 2p^2; it barely helps near p=0.5 and is "much smaller" only for p < 0.191. It ignores Z errors. A Hadamard-wrapped copy protects against Z and fails against X.
- Lab 8: gradient descent lr=0.4 converges from every seed; lr=1.5 never does. Real H2 (STO-3G, 0.735 A): HF -1.11700, FCI -1.13731 Ha, matched by a 1-parameter VQE.
- Lab 9: the error-sum layout heuristic matched the benchmark on only 1 of 3 calibration days; benchmark instead. Exponential ZNE beats linear; a 4th fold point does not help. Dense-unitary Shor costs 876 CNOTs vs 104 compiled and still beats the 0.25 floor (0.39 vs 0.46).
- Lab 10: transpiler does NOT cancel identity cswap pairs (124 vs 81 CNOTs); success hits the 0.25 floor from above near 100x CNOT error; readout mitigation gives a stable +0.0011.
- First real-hardware run (2026-10-13, ibm_fez, 156 qubits, open plan): GHZ(3) fidelity 0.940 +/- 0.011, job took 16 s. Simulated ibm_sherbrooke gave 0.959.
- Surface code (code-capacity noise, MWPM): d=3 exact logical rate 0.0369 at p=0.05; d=3/d=7 curves cross near p=0.094 (literature ~0.103); at d=3 it is worse than Lab 7's repetition code against X noise alone but handles Z too.
- Quantum ML (circles/moons, 150 pts): tuned classical RBF kernel is never beaten (moons: RBF 0.980, ZZ kernel 0.953, product kernel 0.833, 3-layer re-uploading VQC 0.987); re-uploading depth 1->3 raises circles accuracy 0.79->0.97; no quantum advantage claimed.
- Hamiltonian simulation (4-qubit TFIM, J=h=1, t=1): Trotter error slopes -1.01 (first order) and -2.01 (second order); second order costs the same 2(n-1) CNOTs per step. With 1% CNOT depolarizing noise, fidelity peaks at r=4 (first order, 0.806) and r=2 (second order, 0.888), then falls: more Trotter steps is not always better.
- Heisenberg chain (XX+YY+ZZ): 4-site open chain E0 = -3-2*sqrt(3) = -6.4641; a bond gate of three commuting rotations costs 6 CNOTs naively but 3 after resynthesis (level 3); Trotter circuits conserve S_z exactly even for a crude step. HVA from singlet dimers: n=4 exact at p=1, n=6 exact at p=3 (6 angles), n=8 error 0.20 -> 0.027 over p=1..4.
- Lab 15 dry run (fake_sherbrooke, 4 qubits): magnetisation tracks exact within ~0.03 RMS because this observable is forgiving; the k=0 circuit has no gates so it isolates readout error.
- Lab 15 live, fixed-layout rerun (2026-10-13, ibm_fez qubits 144-147, 1000 shots): corrected RMS error vs exact 0.048 over 4 Trotter steps (24 CNOTs); error 0.084 at k=4. Earlier runs on drifting layouts gave 0.039 (4 steps) and 0.053 (6 steps) and are superseded. Device minus ideal Trotter by k=1..4: -0.012, -0.028, +0.008, +0.050 (rises above ideal at depth, which is what T1 decay toward |0> would do, but it is not proven). Magnetisation is a forgiving observable.
- Lab 17 live, clean (2026-10-13, ibm_fez qubits 44,45,37,25, 2000 shots): P0 = 0.977, 0.945, 0.917, 0.887, 0.849 at 0/12/24/36/48 CNOTs; single-exponential fit residual 0.6 sigma; 0.281% error per CNOT, equivalent depolarizing lambda 0.0037, about 2.7x better than Lab 14's assumed 0.01. Fake Sherbrooke dry run: 0.59%.
- Faulty measurements (phenomenological noise, p=q, R=d rounds): every single data or measurement fault on d=3 is corrected, 129 of 741 fault pairs are not; threshold crossing d=3/d=7 near 2.5% (literature ~2.9%) versus 9.4% for Lab 12's perfect-measurement model; decoding with full history gives 0.035 vs 0.106 for last-round-only at d=5, p=q=0.02. Circuit-level noise (hook errors) would lower it to ~1%.
- Lab 17 mirror benchmark: s (decay per CNOT) = 0.75 x depolarizing lambda in simulation, so a measured s maps back to an equivalent lambda; fake_sherbrooke dry run gave 0.57% per CNOT (lambda_eq ~0.0075).
- Lesson from the first live Lab 17 run (ibm_fez): transpiling each circuit separately let the physical qubits change with circuit depth, giving a non-monotone, non-exponential P0 (0.8175 at 36 CNOTs, 0.852 at 48; fit residual 5.3 sigma). Always fix one layout (place the deepest circuit, reuse its qubits via initial_layout) when comparing depths. Labs 15 and 17 now do this.
- Circuit-level surface code (Stim, all four noise knobs = p): d=3/d=7 crossing at ~0.71% (literature ~0.7%); d=3 failure goes as p^1.9; shortest graphlike logical error = d (hook errors cost no distance with this schedule). Threshold ladder: 9.4% (data errors only, Lab 12) -> 2.5% (+noisy readout, Lab 18) -> 0.7% (circuit level, Lab 19).
- Warm-started HVA (Lab 20, Heisenberg, singlet start): n=6 exact at 3 layers; n=8 error 0.338, 0.124, 0.036, 0.025, 0.025 for p=1..5, then 0.0076 (sandbox) or 0.0216 (Mac) at p=6: a plateau at p=4-5, and the p=6 value depends on the platform's optimiser path. 15 CNOTs per layer at n=6 after resynthesis (30 if left at optimisation level 1). With depolarizing noise per CNOT, best depth at n=6 is 3 (noiseless), 2 (0.1%), 1 (0.37%, the ibm_fez level), 1 (1%): more layers hurt on noisy hardware. n=10 is too slow for the dense simulator (>5 min).
- Noise-aware training (Lab 21, n=6): angles optimised under the noise model beat noise-unaware angles at depth 3 (gain >0.05 at 0.37%, >0.1 at 1%), but the best energy over all depths is unchanged within 0.01: training does not rescue deep circuits, one layer is still best at lambda >= 0.0037.
- Union-find decoder (Lab 22): never beats MWPM beyond noise, slightly worse on average; thresholds about 0.085-0.095 (2D code-capacity) and 0.022-0.025 (with faulty measurements). Pure-Python UF costs about 18-40 us/shot (Mac / sandbox) versus about 1-2 us for compiled MWPM, so its advantage is asymptotic scaling in hardware, not Python speed.
- Sparse simulation (Lab 23): E0/n = -1.68747, -1.70321, -1.71403, -1.72192 for n=8,10,12,14, approaching 1-4ln2 = -1.7726 from above; gap 1.571, 1.309, 1.124, 0.985 (closing). Warm-started HVA with 8 layers reaches error/site < 1e-3 only at n=8; n=12 error after 8 layers is 2.2e-2 (Mac) or 4.7e-2 (sandbox), the last layers depend on the platform optimiser path; n=14 is 8.7e-2 (~2 min). Dense H at n=14 is 4 GB; state vector is 0.24 MB. Adjoint gradient matches finite differences to 1e-9.
- MPS (Lab 24): with chi = 2^(n/2) the MPS equals the state vector; at n=16 the energy error is 3.6e-3 (chi=16), 1.6e-5 (32), 1e-8 (64). Trained HVA state keeps half-chain entropy about 0.4 (max 8.3 at n=24), so chi=64 suffices. A depth-p circuit is local, so the bulk energy density is exactly size-independent once n exceeds the light cone (slopes agree to 1e-14 for n=12..64): angles trained at n=12 transfer to any n. Cost is linear in n (n=128 in ~5 s, 15 MB). Bulk error per site by depth 1..6 for trained angles: about 6e-2, 3e-2, 3e-2, 1.6e-2, 1.1e-2, 1.1e-2 (seed and platform dependent; training has plateaus, so 4 starts per depth; on the Mac the n=12 training errors were 4.3e-1 ... 1.8e-2 and the bulk slope was -1.76167).
