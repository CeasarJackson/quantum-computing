## Key facts to remember
- Qiskit is little-endian (qubit 0 is the rightmost bit); Lab 2 is big-endian. `reverse_qargs()` converts.
- Grover N=8 peaks at k=2 (0.9453), overshoots at k=3. Two marked items out of 8: k=1 gives probability exactly 1.
- Shor N=15: every coprime base works with probability 0.5 except a=14 (a^(r/2) = -1, never). With N=21, success is 0 until 2^t > N and is not monotone in t (0.012 at t=5, 0.005 at t=6, 0.31 at t=9).
- Bit-flip code: logical/raw error ratio is 3p - 2p^2; it barely helps near p=0.5 and is "much smaller" only for p < 0.191. It ignores Z errors. A Hadamard-wrapped copy protects against Z and fails against X.
- Lab 8: gradient descent lr=0.4 converges from every seed; lr=1.5 never does. Real H2 (STO-3G, 0.735 A): HF -1.11700, FCI -1.13731 Ha, matched by a 1-parameter VQE.
- Lab 9: the error-sum layout heuristic matched the benchmark on only 1 of 3 calibration days; benchmark instead. Exponential ZNE beats linear; a 4th fold point does not help. Dense-unitary Shor costs 876 CNOTs vs 104 compiled and still beats the 0.25 floor (0.39 vs 0.46).
- Lab 10: transpiler does NOT cancel identity cswap pairs (124 vs 81 CNOTs); success hits the 0.25 floor from above near 100x CNOT error; readout mitigation gives a stable +0.0011.
- First real-hardware run (2026-10-13, ibm_fez, 156 qubits, open plan): GHZ(3) fidelity 0.940 +/- 0.011, job took 16 s. Simulated ibm_sherbrooke gave 0.959.
- Surface code (code-capacity noise, MWPM): d=3 exact logical rate 0.0369 at p=0.05; d=3/d=7 curves cross near p=0.094 (literature ~0.103); at d=3 it is worse than Lab 7's repetition code against X noise alone but handles Z too.
- Quantum ML (circles/moons, 150 pts): tuned classical RBF kernel is never beaten (moons: RBF 0.980, ZZ kernel 0.953, product kernel 0.833, 3-layer re-uploading VQC 0.987); re-uploading depth 1->3 raises circles accuracy 0.79->0.97; no quantum advantage claimed.
- Hamiltonian simulation (4-qubit TFIM, J=h=1, t=1): Trotter error slopes -1.01 (first order) and -2.01 (second order); second order costs the same 2(n-1) CNOTs per step. With 1% CNOT depolarizing noise, fidelity peaks at r=4 (first order, 0.806) and r=2 (second order, 0.888), then falls: more Trotter steps is not always better.
- Heisenberg chain (XX+YY+ZZ): 4-site open chain E0 = -3-2*sqrt(3) = -6.4641; a bond gate of three commuting rotations costs 6 CNOTs naively but 3 after resynthesis (level 3); Trotter circuits conserve S_z exactly even for a crude step. HVA from singlet dimers: n=4 exact at p=1, n=6 exact at p=3 (6 angles), n=8 error 0.20 -> 0.027 over p=1..4.
- Lab 15 dry run (fake_sherbrooke, 4 qubits): magnetisation tracks exact within ~0.03 RMS because this observable is forgiving; the k=0 circuit has no gates so it isolates readout error.
- Lab 15 live, fixed-layout rerun (2026-10-13, ibm_fez qubits 144-147, 1000 shots): corrected RMS error vs exact 0.048 over 4 Trotter steps (24 CNOTs); error 0.084 at k=4. Earlier runs on drifting layouts gave 0.039 (4 steps) and 0.053 (6 steps) and are superseded. Device minus ideal Trotter by k=1..4: -0.012, -0.028, +0.008, +0.050 (rises above ideal at depth, which is what T1 decay toward |0> would do, but it is not proven). Magnetisation is a forgiving observable.
- Lab 17 live, clean (2026-10-13, ibm_fez qubits 44,45,37,25, 2000 shots): P0 = 0.977, 0.945, 0.917, 0.887, 0.849 at 0/12/24/36/48 CNOTs; single-exponential fit residual 0.6 sigma; 0.281% error per CNOT, equivalent depolarizing lambda 0.0037, about 2.7x better than Lab 14's assumed 0.01. Fake Sherbrooke dry run: 0.59%.
- Faulty measurements (phenomenological noise, p=q, R=d rounds): every single data or measurement fault on d=3 is corrected, 129 of 741 fault pairs are not; threshold crossing d=3/d=7 near 2.5% (literature ~2.9%) versus 9.4% for Lab 12's perfect-measurement model; decoding with full history gives 0.035 vs 0.106 for last-round-only at d=5, p=q=0.02. Circuit-level noise (hook errors) would lower it to ~1%.
- Lab 17 mirror benchmark: s (decay per CNOT) = 0.75 x depolarizing lambda in simulation, so a measured s maps back to an equivalent lambda; fake_sherbrooke dry run gave 0.57% per CNOT (lambda_eq ~0.0075).
- Lesson from the first live Lab 17 run (ibm_fez): transpiling each circuit separately let the physical qubits change with circuit depth, giving a non-monotone, non-exponential P0 (0.8175 at 36 CNOTs, 0.852 at 48; fit residual 5.3 sigma). - Circuit-level surface code (Stim, all four noise knobs = p): d=3/d=7 crossing at ~0.71% (literature ~0.7%); d=3 failure goes as p^1.9; shortest graphlike logical error = d (hook errors cost no distance with this schedule). - MPS (Lab 24): with chi = 2^(n/2) the MPS equals the state vector; at n=16 the energy error is 3.6e-3 (chi=16), 1.6e-5 (32), 1e-8 (64). Trained HVA state keeps half-chain entropy about 0.4 (max 8.3 at n=24), so chi=64 suffices. A depth-p circuit is local, so the bulk energy density is exactly size-independent once n exceeds the light cone (slopes agree to 1e-14 for n=12..64): angles trained at n=12 transfer to any n. Cost is linear in n (n=128 in ~5 s, 15 MB). Bulk error per site by depth 1..6 for trained angles: about 6e-2, 3e-2, 3e-2, 1.6e-2, 1.1e-2, 1.1e-2 (seed and platform dependent; training has plateaus, so 4 starts per depth; on the Mac the n=12 training errors were 4.3e-1 ... 1.8e-2 and the bulk slope was -1.76167). Fit of exact E0(n) = e n + b + c/n for n=8..18 gives e = -1.7722 vs Bethe -1.7726. Truncation energy error need not fall monotonically with chi; discarded weight does.
- DMRG (Lab 25): energies E0 = -13.4997 (n=8), -27.6469 (n=16), -55.98926 (n=32), -112.7017 (n=64), equal to Lanczos for n<=16 to 1e-8 with overlap 1. Fit E0(n) = e n + b + c/n over n=16..64 gives e = -1.77255 vs Bethe -1.77259; half-chain entropy slope vs ln n = 0.18 (c/6 = 0.167); open ends dimerise (bond energies -2.61, -1.18, -2.26, ...). chi=16 error 2e-4, chi=32 error 1e-6 at n=32. Pitfalls found: tensordot (BLAS) not 4-operand einsum for environments (1000x), Lanczos needs two reorthogonalisation passes, ARPACK stalls on a near-exact start vector.
- HVA vs DMRG (Lab 26, angles trained at n=12, 6 layers): error above the true ground energy 0.071 (n=16), 0.16 (24), 0.26 (32), 0.46 (48): linear in n, bulk error 0.012-0.013 per site, equal to the central-bond error. Circuit half-chain entropy saturates at 0.50 while the true state grows 0.59 -> 0.79. - Noise on the MPS (Lab 29, depolarizing p per touched qubit per gate layer, 300 trajectories, n=16, error above DMRG): noiseless 0.238 / 0.124 / 0.122 for 2 / 4 / 8 layers; p=1e-4: 0.31 / 0.17 / 0.20; p=1e-3: 0.48 / 0.62 / 1.29; p=3e-3: 1.09 / 1.71 / 3.31. One Pauli error costs about 4-6 energy units (a singlet bond becomes a triplet), so noise dominates quickly: the 8-layer circuit stops beating the 2-layer one near p of 2e-4 per qubit per layer, far below today's 1e-3-level two-qubit gate errors. Trajectories cost the same as a clean MPS run (Pauli = unitary on one physical index, no extra bond dimension); error-free trajectories are skipped exactly; statistical error 1/sqrt(N) confirmed (ratio 2.04 for 4x N); agrees with the exact density matrix at n=6 (1.2 standard errors).

- Zero-noise extrapolation (Lab 30, depolarizing noise, folding lambda = 1, 3, 5): exact density matrix at n=6, p=0.002: raw bias 0.283, linear fit (1,3) 0.013, quadratic fit (1,3,5) 0.0018 (150x smaller). Folding by lambda matches scaling the rate to lambda*p within 1%. Rate x2.5 grows the raw bias x2.45 but the quadratic leftover x6: extrapolation works only at low noise (leftover about p^3). Trajectories, n=16, 4 layers, p=5e-4, N=1000 per scale: raw bias 0.26 (7 standard errors from ideal), mitigated bias 0.15 +- 0.11 (1.4 standard errors, i.e. consistent with ideal); the standard error is 3x larger, so about 10x more shots for equal precision. Takeaway: ZNE trades bias for variance; it is cheap in qubits and expensive in shots.
- Symmetry verification (Lab 31, exact density matrix n=6, p=0.003, 3-layer angles): raw bias 0.42 for every channel; verification leaves 0.149 for depolarizing (removes 65%, acceptance 0.943), 0.42 for dephasing (removes 0%, acceptance 1) and 0.015 for bit-flip type X/Y noise (removes 96%, acceptance 0.916). The projector onto S_z = 0 is built on the MPS as an average of exp(i phi S_z) over M = 2f+1 points (f = number of X/Y errors, or n+1 safely), each rotation a product of single-site phases; the post-selected energy is Tr(H P0 rho)/Tr(P0 rho) because H commutes with S_z; trajectory estimates are ratios of means (delta-method error). With extrapolation: depolarizing raw 0.421 -> verified 0.149 -> extrapolated 0.0037 -> both -0.0014; dephasing: extrapolation 0.0046, verification nothing. Trajectories n=16, 4 layers, p=5e-4, N=1000: raw bias 0.36 +- 0.04, verified 0.14 +- 0.03, 4% of shots discarded, no extra circuits (unlike extrapolation, which needs 3x and 5x deeper circuits and about 10x more shots). Takeaway: use the cheap symmetry check first for the error types it sees, then extrapolate the remainder.
- PennyLane parity classifier (12/4 disjoint splits, 50 steps, five seeds): initialization mode on split 42 gives quantum test accuracy 70% (SD 41) while majority and ridge-linear both score 0% on that partition; partition mode gives quantum 25%, majority 35%, linear 30%. With four test inputs accuracy moves in 25-point steps and a guesser has SD 25 points, so none of this shows a quantum advantage. At 200 steps the circuit gets 100% on all five splits 42-46 (init 46 was a plateau, escaped after ~110 steps), where polynomial ridge averages 40% and 1-NN 0%; the ansatz's CNOT chain computes parity, so this shows a matched structure, not an advantage. Lab 32 checks: circuit matches NumPy to 3e-16, shift gradient matches finite differences to 3e-10.
- Lab 33 (n=6 exact, 4 layers): amplitude damping at 1% per layer biases the energy by 0.95 (linear in the rate, ratio 1.95 for doubled rate); S_z verification leaves 4e-4 of it with 82% acceptance because every jump changes S_z by one unit and K0 conserves S_z, so only no-jump trajectories survive. Coherent errors: a uniform over-rotation is a global rotation of the singlet and is 3x milder than its random twin of equal infidelity (Z and X identical by SU(2)); a frozen non-uniform detuning grows like fold^1.8 under folding (random errors fold^1.0), so linear extrapolation makes it worse (0.105 -> -0.253) and a quadratic fit only partly helps (-0.068); a mis-scaled gate angle is identical at every fold (U(a)U(-a)=1), so folding cannot see it, and its bias is quadratic in the angle error. Long chain (n=16): damping bias 0.66 +- 0.14 -> 4e-6 verified, 88% acceptance.
- Lab 34 (n=6 exact, 4 layers, frozen detuning sd 0.02 rad): random Pauli frames P (x) P around each bond gate (free, because the gate commutes with them; the four frames give the four one-qubit Paulis orthogonal sign patterns) cut the bias from 0.105/0.822/2.033 to 0.031/0.093/0.155 at folds 1/3/5 (3.4x, 8.8x, 13.1x) and make it grow like fold^0.99 instead of fold^1.84, so the straight-line extrapolation leaves 3e-4 where the un-framed one gave -0.253. Caveat: at fold 1 the coherent bias depends on the trained angles (it can be smaller than the framed one); the framed bias is steady, and the gain is in the scaling. Sampled end to end: extrapolated energy within 0.003 +- 0.002 of clean (n=6) and 0.007 +- 0.004 (n=16, 150 frames/fold) where the coherent runs are 0.1 off. Limits (exact): ZZ crosstalk (0.029/0.249/0.653, fold^1.93) and gate-angle errors are unchanged by frames.
- Lab 35 (PEC, depolarizing noise): gamma per location = (3/lambda - 1)/2 with lambda = 1 - 4p/3, about 1 + 2p; gamma_total = gamma^K over K noisy locations (K=120 on 16 sites, 4 layers): 1.27 at p=1e-3, 2.1 at 3e-3, 11 at 1e-2, with a variance cost of gamma_total^2 (1.6x, 4.2x, 124x). Exact on 6 sites: bias 0.567 (p=0.003) and 1.772 (p=0.01) removed to 1e-13, at any depth. Sampled on 16 sites (p=5e-4, 300 samples): PEC -0.007 +- 0.105 against a raw bias of 0.53. Variance lesson: the weights multiply the energy itself (near -20), so the plain weighted mean has an error bar of 0.71; shifting the observable to H - c with c from a 50-run pilot (legal for any c, since E[weight] = 1) cuts it to 0.105. Model error: assuming a rate x times the true one leaves a residual fraction of 1 - x of the raw bias (0.51, 0.205, -0.208 for x = 0.5, 0.8, 1.2); the noise TYPE (dephasing, bit flip, X only, corrected as depolarizing) leaves under 1%, a special property of this singlet-symmetric circuit's energy.
- Lab 36 (S^2 verification, exact density matrices): a Pauli on one qubit is a spin-1 operator, so acting on the singlet it can only reach S=1, and the S-conserving circuit cannot undo that. All 120 single errors on 6 sites (3K, K=40) have S=0 weight 4e-17; S_z verification lets all 40 Z errors through (it catches X and Y only). Pairs of equal Paulis can return to S=0 (weight up to 0.94), the O(p^2) leak. Fitted bias slopes against p: raw 0.99, S_z 1.01, S^2 2.01. At p=1e-3 on 6 sites: raw +0.0306, S_z +0.0103, S^2 -0.00014 (S^2 acceptance 0.961 = (1-p)^K, S_z 0.974). Pure Z (dephasing) noise: S_z accepts everything and removes nothing; S^2 still removes 99%. On 10 sites S^2 beats raw by more than 20x and S_z by more than 2x for under 1.5x more runs. Extrapolation lesson: verified data scale like p^2, so the straight line of Lab 30 makes it worse (p=1e-3: single +5.9e-4, line -1.8e-3) while a quadratic fit through folds 1, 3, 5 gives +7e-5. Limit: on hardware S_z comes free from the bit strings but S^2 needs extra measurement circuits, so this is an upper bound.
